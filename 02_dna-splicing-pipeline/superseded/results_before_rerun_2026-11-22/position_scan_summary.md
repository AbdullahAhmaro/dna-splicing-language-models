# Position scan: the per-position splice classifier applied along whole genes

_Generated by `scripts/11_scan_report.py` on 2026-11-20 03:11 UTC from the scans written by `scripts/10_scan_gene.py`._

**What was done.** A training window labelled "centre is a donor" is a label for one position (base 300), and the pooling chosen on validation reads only the token covering that base and the token just before it. So the trained head is already a per-position classifier. Here the frozen DNA model is run along 15 whole genes on the held-out test chromosomes, and that head is applied at every position to give a donor score and an acceptor score per base. This round adds (1) 12 more genes, for statistical power, with gene-bootstrap confidence intervals on every pooled number, and (2) a head trained with hard negatives (exon interiors, intron interiors and GT/AG decoys from training-chromosome genes).

> **Read this first: class imbalance.** Both heads were trained on windows: the current head on class-balanced ones (about one third each), the hard-negative head on the same windows plus hard negatives, so about half "none". Along these genes, true sites are about **1 in 701 positions** (254 sites in 177,928 scored bases), about 1 in 1,401 for each type. Nothing was re-calibrated for that. A per-window accuracy of 0.94 does not carry over: a small per-position false-positive rate times thousands of negative positions outnumbers the true sites. Top-k and AUROC need no threshold, so they are the fairest comparisons.

## 1. Top-k and AUROC across all genes, with 95% intervals

## 2. Hard negatives

The current head's false positives sit mostly inside exons, because the training negatives were mostly intronic and intergenic. `13_hard_negatives.py` mines negatives that look like what the scan sees, and the same MLP recipe is retrained with them.

### What was mined

Genes come **only from training chromosomes** (chr1, chr2, chr3, chr4, chr5, chr6, chr7, chr8, chr9, chr10, chr11, chr12, chr13, chr14, chr15, chr16, chr17, chr18, chr19, chrX; read from the dataset: train = chr1, chr2, chr3, chr4, chr5, chr6, chr7, chr8, chr9, chr10, chr11, chr12, chr13, chr14, chr15, chr16, chr17, chr18, chr19, chrX, chrY, test = chr20, chr21; chr22 is in neither dataset split; chrY skipped). 75 protein-coding genes were drawn from 60 seeded random 5-Mb regions (canonical transcript protein-coding, 5–60 kb, ≥ 4 exons, no overlapping protein-coding gene), up to 20 windows per group per gene. **15 genes are held out entirely** for evaluation. "Annotated splice site" means an internal exon boundary of any transcript of any gene in the region, on either strand, so no alternative site is used as a negative. Windows are 600 nt of the genome + strand with the position at offset 300, the dataset's own convention.

| group | definition | training genes | held-out genes |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **exon interior** | inside a canonical exon, ≥ 20 bases from every annotated splice site | 1,200 | 300 |
| **intron interior** | inside a canonical intron, ≥ 20 bases from every annotated splice site, outside every annotated exon | 1,200 | 300 |
| **decoy** | GT right after the base or AG right before it in the gene's orientation (where a real donor / acceptor has it), ≥ 10 bases from every annotated splice site | 1,200 | 300 |
| **total** | | **3,600** | 900 |

Decoys: 750 acceptor-like (AG before), 750 donor-like (GT after); 272 in exons, 1,228 in introns. Interior windows also carry the motif by chance: exon interior 180, intron interior 186. Convention check on these genes' own canonical sites: donors 616/620 show the motif at the training offset, acceptors 619/620 show the motif at the training offset. Counts per gene: `results/hard_negatives.json`.

### Training and validation (same recipe, same 07 protocol)

The MLP architecture and training recipe are unchanged (03). Selection follows 07: the same 10% of original training rows, plus ~10% of the hard negatives taken as whole genes, are held out. ### Verdict

**First, the premise.** Exons are 19% of the scored sequence. With the current head at the 0.9 operating point, false positives are: NT-v2-50M donors 27% exonic, 1.5× the intronic density; acceptors 25% exonic, 1.4× the intronic density; DNABERT-2 donors 39% exonic, 2.7× the intronic density; acceptors 25% exonic, 1.4× the intronic density. So exons do carry more false positives per kb than introns, as suspected, but most false positives were intronic all along, simply because introns are most of a gene. Removing every exonic false positive could at best cut the total by that exonic share.

**Did the exonic false-positive problem improve, stay the same, or move elsewhere?**

- **NT-v2-50M:** **Partly improved**, and the false positives did not move into introns: at equal recall (the current head's 0.9 operating point), donors **improved** (exonic ×0.65, intronic ×0.90, total ×0.84; exon:intron density 1.5 → 1.1); acceptors **stayed the same** (exonic ×0.76, intronic ×0.86, total ×0.83; exon:intron density 1.4 → 1.2).

## Composition control

Same idea as section 4 of `motif_analysis.md`, at every position of every gene. The GT/AG rule in transcript orientation proposes every GT as a donor and every AG as an acceptor (10,248 false donor and 13,360 false acceptor candidates). A gate that only sees the base composition of the surrounding 600 nt (the 09 logistic regression) keeps the highest-scoring candidates, per gene, until it has found as many true sites as the model did. That cut-off is set on the genes' own sites, which favours the control. Genes where the model found a site the rule cannot (non-canonical) are *unreachable* and add nothing to the control, which understates it.

## Why a high AUROC still gives a low top-k

An AUROC of 0.99 means about 1% of positions outscore a typical true site. A gene has ~1,401 positions per donor, so roughly a dozen positions per gene score above a typical true donor. That is the class-imbalance effect in one sentence: a good per-window classifier is not yet a good per-position detector.

## Honest limitations

- **Balanced training, imbalanced reality.** True sites are about 1 in 701 positions; neither head was calibrated for that, so thresholded calls still come with hundreds to thousands of false positives.
- **15 genes.** The intervals are from resampling these genes. They cover gene-to-gene variation among genes like these (protein-coding, 4–25 kb, on two chromosomes), not all human genes.
- **Matched-recall thresholds are set on the test genes.** That is fine for comparing the two heads with each other (both get the same advantage), but the thresholds themselves are not a deployable operating point.
- **Hard negatives are labelled from annotation.** An unannotated real site would be a wrong negative; excluding every annotated boundary of every transcript in the region on both strands keeps this rare, not impossible.
- **Resolution.** NT-v2 gets a score per base. DNABERT-2 points to a whole token (~5 bases), so its ±2-base tolerance is effectively wider and its false positives are counted per token.
- **Canonical transcript only.** Real sites of other transcripts inside the span count as false positives; the tables show how many.
