## Does a row label a WINDOW or a POSITION?

**Each row labels an entire WINDOW.** Every row is one 600-nt sequence with exactly one integer label; there is no per-nucleotide label and no column giving a position inside the sequence.

The window is positioned so that the candidate splice junction sits at its centre (around offset 300). The label answers *"is the centre of this window a donor site, an acceptor site, or neither?"*. So the task is **3-way classification of a fixed window**, not **locating** the cut point in a long sequence. The advisor's framing from meeting 3 ("output a number 0 to 8000 for the cut point") is a different, harder task that this dataset does not directly provide.

## What each label value means

**Status: INFERRED.** Label names are NOT documented in the dataset card; they were inferred from canonical-motif enrichment on the training split. The evidence is strong: 98.5% of label-2 rows carry GT (or its reverse complement AC) at one fixed offset, 99.8% of label-1 rows carry AG (or CT), versus at most 13.6% of label-0 rows.

| label | meaning | rows with GT@301 or AC@298 | rows with AG@298 or CT@301 |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 0 | none | 10.0% | 13.6% |
| 1 | acceptor | 4.9% | 99.8% |
| 2 | donor | 98.5% | 3.6% |

Offsets are 0-based positions in the sequence string; `GT@301` means `sequence[301:303] == 'GT'`.

### Important: sequences are NOT strand-normalised

Only about half of the splice-site windows show the textbook motif. Donors: 50.7% have `GT` at offset 301, and 51.5% instead have `AC` at offset 298, which is `GT` read on the opposite strand. Acceptors: 53.3% have `AG` at offset 298, and 52.8% have `CT` at offset 301 (reverse complement of `AG`).

So the windows are written on the genome's + strand, whatever strand the gene is on. Genes on the − strand appear reverse-complemented. Two consequences:

1. A model has to recognise a splice site in both orientations.
2. A rule that only looks for `GT`/`AG` at one offset can catch at most about half of one class. Donor `GT` (offset 301) and acceptor `AG` (offset 298) are also at different offsets. `04_baseline.py` therefore reports the rule exactly as specified *and* a strand-aware variant.

### Other differences between classes

GC fraction by class (train): label 0 (none): 40.0%, label 1 (acceptor): 43.5%, label 2 (donor): 45.4%. Negative windows are noticeably more AT-rich than splice-site windows, so overall base composition alone carries some signal. That is worth knowing when reading the results of a mean-pooled embedding.
